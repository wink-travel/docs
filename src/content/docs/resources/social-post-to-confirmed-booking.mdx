## Why social posts often fail to convert

Many hotel posts create attention but lose booking intent — because the post links to a generic homepage, the booking page doesn't match the offer, the guest has to search again, the promoted offer isn't in the booking path, the wording wasn't approved before posting, mobile checkout is slow, the hotel can't track which post created the click, or the guest is sent to an OTA. The problem is rarely "social doesn't work" — it's that **the post, link, offer, checkout and reporting are disconnected.**

## Social media marketing vs the social-to-booking flow

| Question | Social media marketing | Social-to-booking flow |
|---|---|---|
| Main objective | Awareness, reach, engagement, saves. | Move qualified attention into a bookable hotel path. |
| Approval focus | Brand look and message. | Brand, offer, policy, rate language, image rights and booking route. |
| Link behavior | Generic bio link, homepage or OTA page. | Specific link, card, storefront, QR code or Booking Engine path. |
| Success metric | Likes, comments, views, followers. | Clicks, booking starts, inquiries, completed bookings, attribution. |
| Guest experience | See post, then search again. | See post, click, land on matching offer, book or inquire. |

Social marketing creates demand. Social-to-booking design captures it.

## The six-part loop

**1. Approved content.** Review the image or video, caption, offer name, room or package, dates and availability language, price/value wording, cancellation conditions, disclosure language, link destination, channel and publish date — before it goes live. For paid or incentivized content, the FTC provides U.S. disclosure guidance; check local rules in every market.

**2. Matching offer.** The post should point to the thing it promotes — a family weekend post opens the family weekend route; a wellness post opens the room-first wellness package. The room remains the core product unless a separately approved inquiry or add-on path exists.

**3. Bookable route.** The next step can be a shareable link, QR code, web component, WinkLinks storefront, hotel offer page, Booking Engine route, event inquiry path, partner campaign link or AI handoff. A flash offer, a hotel collection and a conference accommodation page shouldn't all use the same generic link.

**4. Source attribution.** The path should know where the guest came from — channel, post, campaign, creative, partner, QR code, storefront, link, date, market and language. Google's Campaign URL Builder is a public example of campaign tagging; the same principle applies as the guest moves from post to booking.

**5. Booking Engine fulfillment.** Once the guest clicks, Booking Engine loads the correct hotel, dates, room or package context, rates and availability, policies, guest details, payment or inquiry flow, and source/partner context. Social creates the content and attention; Studio and WinkLinks create the bookable assets; **Booking Engine completes the room-first booking or inquiry.**

**6. Confirmation and reporting.** The loop isn't complete until the right parties know what happened — guest, hotel, partner (where a partner route is involved) and platform (where an integration is involved). Reporting should show whether the social route created booking value, not just clicks.

## What the guest should experience

The hotel workflow can be complex; the guest path shouldn't be: see a relevant post → understand the offer → click or scan → land on the matching offer or storefront → see room, package, inclusions and policies → select dates → book or inquire → receive confirmation.

## Before you post

| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Approved creative | Prevents incorrect imagery, rights issues or off-brand visuals. |
| Approved message | Keeps tone, claims and offer language controlled. |
| Offer availability | Routes into real-time Booking Engine data on a Wink path. |
| Room-first booking logic | Keeps spa, dining, experiences and events attached to a stay or inquiry. |
| Policies | Reduces guest confusion before checkout. |
| Disclosure | Helps paid, gifted or partner content follow applicable rules. |
| Link destination | Ensures the post opens the matching route. |
| Tracking | Preserves campaign and source context. |
| Mobile experience | Most social clicks happen on mobile. |

The best social-to-booking work is done before publication — not after the post starts receiving clicks.

## Common mistakes

- **Publishing before approval.** Avoidable brand, rate, policy, image-rights and disclosure risk.
- **Linking everything to the homepage.** Social attention is specific; a generic destination forces a restart.
- **Treating engagement as conversion.** Likes and saves are signals, not bookings.
- **Promoting unavailable offers.** If the post and booking path disagree, trust breaks immediately.
- **Losing partner context.** Partner-led traffic should keep attribution through checkout and reporting.
- **Sending mobile users into desktop friction.** The booking path should be fast and easy on a phone.
