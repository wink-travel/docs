## SEO, AEO and AI search work together

AEO doesn't replace SEO — it extends it. Google's AI features use normal Search systems, and Google says there's no special schema.org markup or AI file required for AI Overviews or AI Mode. The fundamentals still matter: crawlable pages, helpful content, clear structure, descriptive links, useful metadata, accurate structured data and good page experience.

The difference is the output. Traditional SEO aims to earn a click from a results page; AEO also aims to be understood **inside** an answer, comparison, shortlist or assistant response. The page should serve both a human reading the website and an answer engine extracting facts — the best content helps both. Traditional copy ("a unique stay," "perfectly located," "unforgettable experience") sounds pleasant but doesn't help an engine compare two hotels. Answer-engine-ready content is specific: what the hotel is, who it serves, what guests can do, what the rules are, and what to do next.

## The content model answer engines need

| Content layer | What it should answer | Example content |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | What is the hotel and what type of stay? | Boutique city hotel, adults-only resort, family beach resort. |
| Location | Why does this location matter? | Neighborhood, airport distance, transport, attractions, beach access. |
| Guest fit | Who is it best for? | Families, couples, business, wellness, events, groups, long-stay. |
| Rooms | What are the real differences? | Size, view, bedding, occupancy, balcony, accessibility, inclusions. |
| Amenities | What can guests actually use? | Pool, spa, gym, restaurant, kids club, coworking, parking, transfers. |
| Policies | What affects the booking decision? | Cancellation, payment, children, pets, check-in, accessibility. |
| Offers | What specific stay value is available? | Breakfast package, weekend stay, wellness stay, long-stay offer. |
| Media | What proof builds trust? | Room images, maps, dining and spa photos with captions and alt text. |
| Booking route | What action comes next? | Room booking, package, inquiry, partner storefront, Booking Engine. |

## How to write direct answers

Answer engines work well with content that gives the answer first, then explains: a question-led heading → one direct answer paragraph → supporting details → a table or bullets → a booking route. Compare:

## Structured data: useful, not magic

Structured data helps systems understand page meaning — it does **not** guarantee AI visibility, citations, rich results or rankings. Use it only when it accurately reflects visible content.

| Page type | Useful schema | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hotel page | Hotel, LodgingBusiness, LocalBusiness, Organization, BreadcrumbList | Only facts that are visible and approved. |
| Room page | HotelRoom; Product/Offer only where approved | Avoid unstable public price markup. |
| Offer page | Offer only where public terms are approved | Don't mark up unapproved discounts. |
| Resource article | Article, BreadcrumbList, FAQPage | FAQ schema must match visible content. |
| FAQ section | FAQPage | Visible questions and answers only. |

Accurate, visible and maintainable beats ambitious markup that goes stale.

## Internal links, anchor text and images

Answer engines need relationships between pages. Use crawlable anchor links with useful text — "View family rooms" beats "click here"; "Book the wellness package" beats "learn more." Link overview pages to room pages, room pages to Booking Engine paths, location pages to relevant offers, and partner storefronts to the right hotel paths.

Hotels are visual, but AI still needs text context around images. Give each important image a descriptive filename and alt text — "Deluxe king room with city view and work desk," not "room." Don't rely on text embedded inside an image: if the room name, inclusion or policy matters, publish it as text too.

## AEO content checklist

| Checklist item | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Clear page purpose | Can a reader tell what this page answers in seconds? |
| Direct answer | Is the main question answered near the top? |
| Entity clarity | Are name, location, category and audience clear? |
| Room clarity | Are room differences specific and useful? |
| Policy clarity | Are cancellation, payment and check-in rules visible? |
| Crawlable links | Are booking links real anchor links with descriptive text? |
| Image context | Do images have alt text, captions or surrounding text? |
| Structured data | Does schema match visible, approved content? |
| Freshness | Are dates, offers and policies current? |
| Booking route | Does the content lead to the correct booking or inquiry path? |

## Common mistakes

- **Writing generic content.** Specific facts, guest fit and practical details beat lifestyle language.
- **Hiding answers below marketing copy.** Answer the family-friendly question before the lifestyle story.
- **Using schema that doesn't match visible content.** Structured data should reinforce visible truth, not introduce hidden claims.
- **Treating FAQ as decoration.** Answer real questions; don't add thin blocks just for markup.
- **Publishing stale offers.** Answer engines can surface outdated content and confuse guests.
- **Letting AI invent hotel facts.** Room size, occupancy, amenities and policies aren't creative writing — review them.
- **Forgetting the booking route.** A page that answers intent but doesn't lead to a booking or inquiry path leaks demand.
