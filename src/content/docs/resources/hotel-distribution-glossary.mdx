## Channels & demand

### Direct booking
A reservation made through a route the hotel controls — its website, a booking link, a QR code, a storefront, a partner route or an AI handoff — rather than through an OTA that owns the guest relationship. The transaction itself.

### Direct distribution
The whole system that creates direct bookings: content, links, partner routes, storefronts, campaigns, rate and availability, booking engine, attribution and confirmation. Broader than "the hotel website." See [Direct Hotel Distribution](/resources/direct-hotel-distribution/).

### OTA (Online Travel Agency)
A third-party marketplace (e.g. the large booking platforms) that lists hotels and sells rooms, usually for a commission of 15–25%. Useful for reach; costly for margin and guest ownership. See [Direct Bookings vs OTA Economics](/resources/direct-bookings-vs-ota-economics/).

### Metasearch
A price-comparison surface (including Google's hotel results) that aggregates rates from OTAs and direct sources so travelers can compare and click through to book.

### Free booking links
Unpaid slots in Google's hotel results that let a hotel show its own rate and send the traveler to its booking page — no click cost. See [Google Free Booking Links](/resources/google-free-booking-links/).

### Partner channel
A measurable demand route created by an agency, KOL, event organiser, DMC, community, publisher, bank, loyalty program or platform that influences where travelers stay. See [Hotel Partner Channel](/resources/hotel-partner-channel/).

### Social commerce
Turning social attention — posts, bio links, QR codes, partner recommendations — into measurable, bookable paths. See [Hotel Social Commerce](/resources/hotel-social-commerce/).

## Economics & payment

### Commission
A percentage of a booking paid to the channel that produced it. OTA commissions commonly sit in the 15–25% range; Wink's all-in cost on a confirmed direct booking is 5.5%. See [Pricing](/pricing/).

### All-in cost
The total cost of a confirmed booking including payment processing. On Wink, 5.5% = 1.5% Wink platform fee + 4.0% payment fee, with no separate processor contract.

### Attribution
Knowing which source, campaign, link, storefront, partner or agent created a booking. Wink preserves attribution for 6 months per click — credited to the booking, not just the session.

### Merchant of record
The party legally responsible for the transaction and funds. Under Wink's standard model, Wink acts as merchant of record; licensed travel agencies can instead act as merchant of record themselves. See [Pricing](/pricing/).

### Payment
Wink handles booking payment as the merchant of record — checkout, cancellation-policy logic, tokenized cards, 3DS, multi-currency, split payouts and settlement. Funds release to the hotel at guest check-in, after Wink's fee. Bundled into the 5.5% all-in; no separate processor contract.

### Average booking value (ABV)
The average total value of a booking. It rises when a booking engine sells the whole stay — packages, add-ons, dining, spa, experiences — not just the room.

## Supply & connectivity

### ARI (Availability, Rates and Inventory)
The live data a hotel must provide before a guest can book: what's available, at what price, with what restrictions. See [Connectivity](/resources/connectivity/).

### Channel manager
Software that distributes a hotel's ARI to connected channels and syncs bookings back. Wink connects with 11 channel managers including SiteMinder, Cloudbeds, RateGain and D-EDGE.

### PMS (Property Management System)
The hotel's operational system of record for reservations, rooms and guests. Bookings can sync to it through connectivity.

### CRS (Central Reservation System)
A system that centralizes rates, availability and reservations across a hotel or group; Wink supports SynXis as a CRS.

### Extranet
A hotel's control layer for supply: profile, rooms, rates, availability, policies, promotions, channels and booking-engine configuration. On Wink, the single source of truth. See [Extranet](/products/extranet/).

### Booking Engine
The fulfillment layer that turns approved supply into a confirmed booking — loading the right room, rate and add-ons, taking payment, and confirming across every route. See [Booking Engine](/products/booking-engine/).

## AI, content & developers

### AI discovery
How travelers increasingly find hotels through AI assistants and answer engines instead of scanning links. See [AI Discovery for Hotels](/resources/ai-discovery-hotels/).

### Answer engine
An AI system that responds with a direct answer rather than a list of links (AI Overviews, ChatGPT search, assistants). Hotels need structured, current content to be understood. See [Hotel Content for Answer Engines](/resources/hotel-content-answer-engines/).

### AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)
Structuring hotel content — facts, rooms, policies, offers, booking routes — so answer engines can understand, compare and cite it. The AI-era companion to SEO.

### Agentic commerce
AI agents that don't just recommend a hotel but transact — reading live rates, creating bookings, taking payment and reacting to events. See [AI Agents Booking Hotels](/resources/ai-agents-hotel-booking/) and [Agentic AI](/agentic-ai/).

### MCP (Model Context Protocol)
A standard that lets AI agents read a system's live API contracts. Wink hosts an MCP server so agents can discover operations and build working integrations without guessing at endpoints.

### Web Components
Embeddable, bookable hotel modules — cards, maps, grids — that drop live inventory into any website or product, with Booking Engine handling the booking.

### Webhooks
Event notifications a system sends when something happens (e.g. `booking.created`). Wink exposes 64+ webhook events so products can react in real time.
