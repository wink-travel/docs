## The macro picture: OTAs are gaining, not fading

Independent hotels are *more* dependent on online travel agencies than a year ago, not less. In Cloudbeds' **2026 State of Independent Hotels** report — built on roughly **90 million bookings across 180 countries** — OTAs accounted for **63.4% of independent-hotel bookings in 2025, up from 61.3% in 2024**, reaching **76.5% in EMEA** and **52.7% in North America**. Direct sits near **36.6% globally** — below the **40–50% "healthy mix"** most commercial teams target.

That dependence is expensive. Independent hotels typically pay **15–30% OTA commission** — Booking.com around 15–18%, Expedia 18–22% base — climbing past **25% effective** once preferred-placement and sponsored-listing programs are layered on.

And direct isn't only cheaper, it's higher quality. The same report found OTA cancellation rates of **21.8% — more than double the 10.6%** on direct bookings. Every point of OTA mix a hotel shifts back to direct compounds: lower commission, fewer cancellations, and a guest relationship the hotel actually owns.

## Demand is moving to feeds and AI answers

How travelers *discover* hotels is shifting faster than the booking split itself:

- **Social is the new top of funnel.** An estimated **84–90% of Gen Z use social platforms for travel inspiration**. On TikTok alone, around **60% treat it as their primary inspiration source** and **~40% say they have booked a trip directly because of it** — and roughly **40% of Gen Z reach for TikTok over Google** to find hotels.
- **AI is now a discovery surface.** About **40% of travelers used AI tools to plan a trip in 2025**. ABTA's *Holiday Habits 2025–26* shows the share using AI for inspiration **doubled (4% → 8%) in a year**; close to **1 in 5 travelers now start planning with AI**, and roughly two-thirds use it to find hotels and flights.

The implication for hotels: demand increasingly *originates* on surfaces the hotel does not own — a feed, an answer engine, a creator's post — but the booking still has to resolve somewhere. The strategic question is whether it resolves on a route the hotel controls, or one it rents back at 15–25%.

## Panel findings: a 12-month cohort study

**Methodology.** A 12-month panel of 9 independent boutique hotels (~500 keys total) running their full direct distribution stack on Wink. The panel tracks **confirmed bookings only** — not traffic, impressions or estimates.

| Metric | Finding |
|---|---|
| Confirmed bookings generated | 4,500+ |
| Average booking value | $200 USD |
| Direct-booking uplift range | +7% to +25%, by feature depth |
| Share via social (active social users) | ~50% of incremental direct bookings |
| Share via partner channels (where partners connected) | At least 1 in 3 bookings |

## Direct distribution is a compound stack

The +7% to +25% range isn't random — it tracks feature depth. Hotels using only Booking Engine and Extranet sit at the lower end; hotels adding Social, WinkLinks, active partner relationships and Traveliko climb toward the top. This is the central finding of the panel: **direct distribution is not a single feature — it's a compound stack, and each layer adds bookable surface area** on exactly the social and AI surfaces where discovery is now happening.

## The OTA economics comparison

The panel hotels were paying **15–25% OTA commission** per booking before Wink. At **5.5% all-in** (1.5% Wink + 4.0% TripPay gateway, powered by Stripe), the delta compounds with volume:

| Annual booking value | 5.5% all-in cost | 15% OTA cost | Kept by the hotel |
|---|---|---|---|
| $500,000 | $27,500 | $75,000 | **$47,500** |
| $1,000,000 | $55,000 | $150,000 | **$95,000** |
| $2,000,000 | $110,000 | $300,000–$500,000 | **$190,000–$390,000** |

And the saving is only the visible part. Direct also means the guest email, the lower cancellation rate, and the ability to bring that guest back without paying an OTA a second time.

## Channel-mix observations

Hotels running multiple demand routes — direct website, Social content, WinkLinks and at least one active partner — saw the strongest booking diversification; hotels relying on a single route (website only) saw the weakest uplift. **Social is the channel most under-used relative to its potential:** hotels with active Social workflows saw roughly half their incremental direct bookings originate from social posts — consistent with the broader shift of discovery into feeds. Partner channels — KOL agencies, event organisers, DMCs, travel communities — contributed **at least 1 in 3 confirmed bookings** wherever a partner relationship was active.

## Caveat & method

The Wink panel is 9 hotels — a cohort, not a statistically representative industry sample — and results reflect varying feature adoption across hotels with full platform access. The industry figures above come from third-party reports (cited below) and are used for context, not extrapolated to Wink. A broader Wink industry survey (Edition 2) is in preparation; survey-based market-share claims are held back until methodology review and approval.
