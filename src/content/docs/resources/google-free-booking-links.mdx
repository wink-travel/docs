## What free booking links are

When a traveler looks at a hotel on Google, they see a price comparison module. For years those slots were paid ads. **Free booking links** added unpaid slots in the same module — so a hotel (or an approved booking partner) can show its own rate and send the traveler directly to its booking page, with no click cost.

For independent and boutique hotels this is meaningful: it's a high-intent surface — the traveler has already chosen the hotel and is comparing where to book — and the direct rate gets to sit next to the OTAs without an ad budget.

Free booking links are not a replacement for a hotel's website, social, partner or AI routes. They're one more **direct distribution** surface — see the broader picture in [Direct Hotel Distribution](/resources/direct-hotel-distribution/).

## How a hotel becomes eligible

Free booking links work off the same hotel data Google uses for its price comparison: **current room rates and availability**, mapped to the correct property. In practice a hotel needs three things working together.

| Requirement | What it means |
|---|---|
| A price feed | Accurate, current rates and availability supplied to Google for the property. |
| Property mapping | The hotel correctly matched to its Google listing so prices attach to the right place. |
| A bookable landing page | The link must resolve to a page that can take the dates, load the rate and complete the booking. |

The price feed is the part most hotels can't do alone — it's typically supplied through a **connectivity or channel partner that participates in Google's free booking links program**, or through Google's own integration options. Check with your channel manager or booking provider about which path they support.

## Why the destination decides the outcome

A free booking link is only as good as where it lands. The most common failure isn't getting the link — it's wasting it:

- The link drops the guest on a generic homepage instead of the room and dates they were comparing.
- The rate shown in Google doesn't match the rate on the page.
- The booking flow is clunky on mobile, where most of this traffic is.
- The path only sells a room, when the guest would have added more.

This is where the booking engine matters. The traveler arriving from a free booking link is ready — the job is to load the right room, rate and dates and let them finish.

## Where Wink fits

Wink doesn't replace Google's program — it gives the click a destination that converts.

- **Accurate supply.** [Extranet](/products/extranet/) is the single source of truth for rooms, rates, availability, policies and packages — connected to a channel manager/PMS or maintained manually — so the data behind a direct rate stays current.
- **A booking flow that finishes the job.** [Booking Engine](/products/booking-engine/) loads the right room and rate, sells the whole stay (packages, add-ons, dining, spa, experiences where configured), takes payment, and confirms the booking to the guest and hotel.
- **Direct economics.** A confirmed direct booking through Wink is **5.5% all-in** versus 15–25% typical OTA commission — the whole reason a free direct link beats sending the same guest to an OTA. See [Pricing](/pricing/) and [Direct Bookings vs OTA Economics](/resources/direct-bookings-vs-ota-economics/).
- **One supply, many routes.** The same Extranet supply also feeds your website, [Social](/products/social/), [WinkLinks](/products/winklinks/) storefronts, partners, [Traveliko](/products/traveliko/) and AI — so a free booking link is one surface in a connected direct stack, not a one-off.

> For the price feed itself, confirm program support with your connectivity or channel partner. Wink's contribution is the accurate supply and the booking destination the link points to.

## A practical setup checklist

1. **Claim and verify** your hotel's Google Business Profile and confirm the property is correctly listed in Google's hotel surfaces.
2. **Confirm feed support** — ask your channel manager, CRS or booking provider whether they support Google's free booking links and how to enable the price feed.
3. **Keep rates and availability accurate** in [Extranet](/products/extranet/) (connected or manual) so the direct price Google shows is real and bookable.
4. **Point the link at a bookable destination** — a Booking Engine flow that accepts dates and completes the reservation, not a static homepage.
5. **Make it mobile-first** — most hotel comparison traffic is on a phone.
6. **Measure** — track the bookings that arrive from free booking links and compare net value against the OTA alternative.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating it as "set and forget."** If the feed breaks or rates go stale, the direct price disappears or misleads.
- **Sending the click to the homepage.** Land on the room and dates the guest was comparing.
- **Mismatched prices.** The rate in Google must equal the rate at checkout, or trust (and the booking) is lost.
- **Ignoring the economics.** The point of a free direct link is keeping the guest at 5.5% instead of paying 15–25% — measure that delta.
